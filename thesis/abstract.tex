\chapter{Abstract}

Ensuring consistency in distributed systems, particularly in Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs), poses significant challenges, especially in environments prone to Byzantine faults. Collaborative editing systems such as peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms exemplify these challenges, where nodes are vulnerable to malicious behavior and data inconsistencies due to transient disconnections. Designing such data types is non‑trivial as subtle choices in the semantics of operations can lead to unintended divergence. Formal verification provides a principled way to address these complexities by proving that a CRDT satisfies properties like convergence and commutativity. Existing work shows how individual CRDTs can be verified using theorem provers.

Our proposal offers a structured approach for CRDT verification by developing a modular framework in the Athena proof assistant. The framework facilitates the specification of both the syntax and operational semantics of CRDTs and guides the proof of key properties such as convergence, commutativity, and idempotence. By identifying and encapsulating common patterns in CRDT design and verification. It is suited to developers who are exploring or prototyping new CRDT variants and need a precise and checkable way to reason about their correctness. While the framework does not cover all possible CRDT designs. It takes a modest step toward making formal verification more approachable in this domain.
