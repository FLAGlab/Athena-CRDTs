8Natural number orderings
IN THIS chapter we return to the study of proof methods for propertie s of the natural
number data type. In Chapter 3, we concentrated on equality p roperties, but here we turn
toordering properties and proof methods suitable for dealing with them . In the process,
we will be putting into further practice some of the methods w e studied in the previous
chapters. These ordering properties also prepare us for dea ling with integer addition and
subtraction in Chapter 9.
We start by axiomatically deﬁning a “strictly-less-than” o perator, <, on natural num-
bers, and from it and equality easily obtain deﬁnitions of ot her commonly used ordering
operators ( ≤,>,≥). Although only a few axioms are required to deﬁne <, there are many
other properties that can be proved as theorems about it and t he other ordering operators.
Subtraction of natural numbers is closely related to their o rdering, so we include an
axiomatization and a few theorems about this operator. Thes e results will be used in deﬁn-
ing integer addition and subtraction in Chapter 9 and for deﬁ ning quotient and remainder
operators in Chapter 13.
We also introduce ordered lists of natural numbers, a subset of the list type we studied in
Chapter 3. This important subset is deﬁned with the aid of a pr edicate on lists, a technique
also used in the ﬁnal section of the chapter to deﬁne binary search trees as a subset of
abinary tree data type. With binary trees that have natural number values in their nodes
we can take advantage of the natural number ordering propert ies to achieve more efﬁcient
searching than with the linear list data type we studied in Ch apter 3, as we will see in detail
in Chapter 11.1But ﬁrst we have to deﬁne binary trees and introduce a corresp onding

lists in having two induction hypotheses , one for each of a binary tree node’s subtrees—and
use it in proving several important properties about binary trees, including the concept of
an “inorder tree walk.” The chapter then concludes with the d eﬁnition of the binary search
tree subset and the proof of an elegant theorem connecting bi nary search trees, inorder tree
walks, and ordered lists.
